§ 05Writing

Articles in preparation

Five short pieces for school leaders, each developing one part of Adaptive by Design. None is published yet; this page will link to each as it appears.

No. 01
Forthcoming

An AI strategy is an inclusion strategy

Ofsted judges AI only by its effect on pupils and now grades inclusion on its own. This piece argues that a school should write one strategy, not two: start where the school will be judged, with pupils who face barriers, and let the choice of tools follow.

Draws on: Adaptive by Design, §01–03

No. 02
Forthcoming

Draw the boundary before the permissions

Most AI policies list what staff may do. A position starts with what AI is not for: judging pupils, predicted grades, deciding whether a pupil has SEN, the content of an EHCP. Why naming the boundary is what makes everything else defensible to parents, governors and inspectors.

Draws on: Adaptive by Design, §05

No. 03
Forthcoming

Pre-teach, gate, fade: adaptive teaching without lowering the ceiling

The SENSE study shows how differentiation turns into easier work. A practical account of a four-step model in which the level changes how much help is visible, never the question, and of what AI does and does not do to make it sustainable.

Draws on: Adaptive by Design, §03–04

No. 04
Forthcoming

Measures that can return bad news

Use is not impact. Six measures for an AI rollout, each against a baseline, two of them designed to embarrass the person proposing them, including a check for cognitive offloading while the research on it is still being done.

Draws on: Adaptive by Design, §07

No. 05
Forthcoming

The second draft for a novice

If AI drafts every resource, early-career teachers lose the practice that makes them expert. Used well, it can spread expert practice instead. On protecting the routes into expertise, and on deciding where the time AI saves should go.

Draws on: Adaptive by Design, §06

Until these appear, the full argument is in the report: Adaptive by Design (PDF).
