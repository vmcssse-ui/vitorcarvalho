§ 07Pre-registered · 17 October 2026

Evaluation, registered in advance

The six measures by which the Adaptive by Design pilot will be judged, published before any data exists. The baseline and result columns are empty on purpose. They will be filled in, whatever they say.

Why publish first

Measures that cannot be chosen after the result

Ofsted's guidance on AI, revised on 21 October 2025, is candid that there is not yet enough evidence to say what good AI use in schools looks like. The DfE's 2024–25 technology survey found that only 35% of school leaders had any way of telling whether their technology was effective. In that position, a year with no problems reported is not a year that went well; it is a year with no honest evaluation.

Registering the evaluation in advance, in public, is the plainest protection against the temptation that follows. Once results exist, it is easy to find the measure that flatters them. So the measures, the way each is taken, the threshold that would count as failure and the decision that follows are written down here, dated, before the baseline is taken. Two of the six were designed to be able to embarrass the person proposing them. That is the point of them.

The six measures are those in section 07 of Adaptive by Design. The pilot they measure is the one at Harlington School in 2026–27: the exemplar units on the four-step model and the SEND booklets, each built from a department's own lesson decks and each awaiting that department's specialist review before it is used with pupils.

The register

Six measures, two empty columns

Each measure is taken against a baseline. A threshold is the result that would count as failure, written now so that it cannot be moved later. The decision rule says what follows from each kind of result: scale the practice, adapt it, or stop it.

Two of the six, the offloading check and staff time, can return bad news by design. The teacher-attention measure watches for the failure the SENSE study found: support that takes pupils with EHCPs away from the class teacher. The common-destination walk watches for the failure the EEF warns of: differentiation that becomes lowered demand.

The data

What the platform now records for these

Since 17 October 2026 the unit pages record a small set of anonymous events. Nothing identifying is stored or sent: no names, no accounts, no free text. A pupil may enter a class code of four to six characters; without one, and without a dashboard address set by the school, everything stays on the pupil's own device and the dashboard page says so.

Each event the engine sends is of the form: class code, unit, chapter, type (gate, module, voice, return or explain), value, level and time. The dashboard reads them back by class code only. This is the position set out under Governance in the strategy: public pages have no accounts, forms or pupil identifiers, and any change to that goes to the data protection officer first.

Change log

Amended only by addition

Every change to this page is a dated entry below. The measures, thresholds and decision rules above are never edited. If a measure proves unworkable, that is recorded here with the reason, and the original row stays as it was written.

The rule for this page

This page will only ever be amended by adding dated entries to the change log and by filling the two empty columns when their time comes. The measures are not re-worded, re-weighted or removed after the fact. A reader comparing this page with an archived copy from 17 October 2026 should find the table unchanged except for the baseline and result columns.

Accountability

Who holds me to it

A measure that only its author can read is a private note. These three groups receive the results, including the ones that count as failure, and are the people entitled to ask why a decision rule was not followed.

Governors

The report at the end of term

The strategy's seventh recommendation is to report to governors what did not work alongside what did. This page gives governors the thresholds in advance, so the report can be checked against them rather than written around them. The question they are owed in July is the one the strategy sets up: where did the time go?

The SENCo

The measures about pupils with EHCPs

Teacher attention is a measure of what happens to pupils with EHCPs when adaptive material enters a lesson. The SENCo is party to the boundary and the tiers from the start, as the strategy's first ninety days require, takes the teacher-attention baseline with me and sees its result before it goes to governors. The boundary stands throughout: AI does not decide whether a pupil has SEN or write the content of an EHCP.

Heads of department

The subjects whose units are used

Every unit was built from a department's own lesson decks, and each is pilot material until that department's specialist has reviewed it. The heads of department whose units are taught with see the gate and completion data for their classes on the dashboard, and their answer to one question in July, whether the pages are theirs, is itself one of the six measures.
