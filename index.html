Senior leadership·AI strategy·Inclusion

An AI strategy is an inclusion strategy.

Ofsted now judges AI only by its effect on pupils, and inspects inclusion as an area in its own right. I lead schools' use of AI from that starting point: adaptive teaching made deliberate, a clear line on what AI is not for, oversight that follows consequence, and an evaluation agreed before rollout that is able to return bad news.

Built and running · October 2026

The case, in three numbers

Built at Harlington School, a London 11–18 comprehensive, by one head of department. It shows the model can be built and run. Whether it raises attainment has not yet been tested; the evaluation is designed to find out.

222booklets

SEND booklets in seven departments

2,080 lessons and 4,160 pages in English, Maths, Science, Geography, History, French and Spanish, each built from the department's own lesson decks. Nothing appears that is not on the board.

25units

A complete A-level course portal

Philosophy, Ethics and Christianity: 87 taught lessons, reached from one address on any device without a login, with decks, booklets and retrieval practice behind each unit.

0pupil records

No pupil data enters any AI tool

By design, not by promise. AI drafts from department decks and anonymised context sheets that list needs and strategies, never names. Public pages have no accounts, forms or analytics.

The measures that will judge this work are registered in advance, with their baseline columns empty, and the errors found so far are logged in public under What went wrong.

2025–26 guidance

What schools are now asked to show

Between October 2025 and September 2026 the rules for AI in English schools moved from permission to expectation. None of the documents tells a school to use AI. Together they say how its use will be judged.

Inspection

Impact on pupils, and inclusion graded on its own

Ofsted does not evaluate AI use as such; it looks at its effect on pupils' education and at how risks are managed. The renewed framework makes inclusion a separate evaluation area.

SEND reform

A universal offer, written down

The February 2026 white paper moves most support into the teaching every pupil can expect, adds school-written individual support plans, and asks each school for an annual inclusion strategy.

Safeguarding

AI inside safeguarding, not beside it

KCSIE 2026 brings AI into the annual filtering and monitoring review. The DfE's product safety standards, extended in January 2026, are the checklist for any pupil-facing tool.

The gap is real: only 9% of state school teachers reported a school-wide AI strategy, against 27% in private schools, and a year later 41% of teachers said they had received no AI training or support at all. Without a strategy, the benefits flow to those already best placed to use them.

Where the time goes is a leadership decision

AI frees time only if someone decides how it is spent. The strategy names the destination in advance: pre-teaching groups, feedback on extended writing, and the termly review of pupils on SEN Support.
