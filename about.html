Professional biography

Dr Vitor Carvalho

PGCE, University of Oxford · MA, University of Trinity Saint David · PhD, University of Nottingham

Dr Vitor Carvalho is Head of Philosophy, Religious Education and Lead on the Extended Project Qualification (EPQ) at Harlington School, an 11–18 comprehensive in the London Borough of Hillingdon, where he has taught since 2018. He teaches Religious Studies, Philosophy and Social Sciences from Key Stage 3 to Key Stage 5, including A-level Philosophy (AQA 7172) and A-level Religious Studies (AQA 7062). He leads a department of six, five of whom he trained from trainee teacher onwards.

His current work is on artificial intelligence as a means of inclusion. Largely outside the duties of his post, he designed and built the Edu.Ai Ecosystem framework, which Harlington uses for a complete A-level course portal and for SEND booklets across seven departments. His strategy report, Adaptive by Design (October 2026), sets out how a school can write its AI strategy as part of its inclusion strategy, with a fixed boundary, oversight tiered by consequence and an evaluation able to return bad news.

He holds a PGCE from the University of Oxford, an MA from the University of Trinity Saint David and a PhD from the University of Nottingham, where his doctoral research was on early Christianity. He has completed the National Professional Qualification in Leading Teacher Development, is completing the NPQ for Senior Leadership (Easter 2027), and plans to take the Oxford Artificial Intelligence Programme at Saïd Business School after it.

In my own words

Why I came to AI through inclusion

I came to AI through a classroom problem, not a technology. In a class of thirty, some pupils arrive without the words the lesson depends on, some can do the task once one step is shown, and some need no help at all. Good teachers adapt for all three. The hard part is doing it every lesson, in every subject, without lowering what anyone is expected to do.

AI makes that affordable, on two conditions: people keep every decision about individual pupils, and the school agrees in advance how it will know whether the work has helped. That is the work I want to lead at senior level.

Career

Harlington School, 2018 to present

An 11–18 comprehensive with a specialist resource provision and a designated unit for autistic pupils.

  1. 2025 – present

    Whole-school adaptive teaching programme

    NPQSL project. Designed and began implementing a whole-school adaptive teaching programme, and built the framework for evaluating it across all subjects. SEND booklets for seven departments, built from each department's own decks, are its first product.

  2. 2023 – present

    Head of Philosophy, Religious Education and Lead on EPQ

    Leads a department of six; conducts appraisal, target-setting and progress review for every member of the team; holds the curriculum budget. Selects specifications, writes schemes of work and sequences the curriculum across Core RE, A-level RS, A-level Philosophy and the EPQ. GCSE results in line with the national average and 11 percentage points above the school average for core subjects, despite a 32% reduction in GCSE teaching time over two years.

  3. June 2023 – July 2024

    Research lead: relationships and sex education

    Led and coordinated a 12-month RSE research project at Harlington with the University of Exeter and the Department of Public Health, Environments and Society, with responsibility for communications, governance, staff training and the timetabling of ten staff.

  4. 2018 – 2023

    Teacher of Religious Education and Philosophy

    Taught RE and Philosophy across Key Stages 3 to 5, contributing to curriculum development and assessment design.
