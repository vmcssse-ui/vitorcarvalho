§ 03Leading SEND

High-quality teaching for pupils with SEND is high-quality teaching, made more deliberate

The EEF's central message, and the starting point for this work. Three strands carry it from a principle to something a school can run and inspect: research-informed teaching, systems of implementation, and governance.

Three strands

From research to a system to account

The renewed inspection framework asks how a school identifies and supports pupils with SEND, disadvantaged pupils and children known to social care. In practice it is the question a head of department asks on a learning walk, put to the whole school: is this lesson reaching the pupil in the corner who is not putting a hand up?

Strand 1

Research-informed teaching

  • The EEF's "five-a-day": explicit instruction, cognitive and metacognitive strategies, scaffolding, flexible grouping and technology to support them.
  • The SENSE study (Webster and Blatchford): when adaptation is improvised, pupils with EHCPs spend more time away from the class teacher and differentiation becomes easier work. Support must add to the teacher's work with the pupil, never replace it.
  • Retrieval practice: a pupil who recalls before being helped is doing the thinking that cognitive offloading would take away.
Strand 2

Systems of implementation

  • A universal offer, written down. The 2026 white paper moves most support into the teaching every pupil can expect; schools that write it down now will find the change an update, not a rebuild.
  • Build once, centrally. Departments supply the curriculum thinking; one team builds to one standard from their own decks.
  • Train the practice, not the tool. Pre-teaching, gating and fading scaffolds, rehearsed in each teacher's own subject, following the EEF's explore, prepare, deliver, sustain.
Strand 3

Governance

  • A fixed boundary. AI does not decide whether a pupil has SEN, write the content of an EHCP, or offer emotional or mental-health support.
  • Oversight by consequence, with named signatories and a record.
  • Measures agreed first, including teacher attention for pupils with EHCPs, and a report to governors on what did not work alongside what did. Governors can then ask: where did the time go?
The adaptive pages

Four steps, one destination

Each exemplar unit is built as a read-along ebook for pre-teaching and a set of adaptive modules. The level a pupil works at changes how much help is visible, never the question they finish on.

1 Pre-teach

Before the lesson, the pupil hears and reads the words and prior knowledge it will rely on, in a read-along ebook where the text is highlighted as the audio plays.

2 Gate

A short check of two or three questions places the pupil at a starting level. Recall comes before any help is shown.

3 · Support

The steps and a worked example printed beside the task.

3 · Core

The steps hidden behind one hint.

3 · Stretch

No steps; the pupil explains and extends.

4 One destination

Every pupil finishes on the same question. As modules progress the scaffold fades, so support is a route in, never a lower ceiling.

The level changes how much help is visible, never the question.

A worked example

On a Tuesday, in a Year 8 science lesson

A class will meet series circuits on Thursday. On Tuesday, four pupils identified by the department's pre-assessment, not by the SEND register, open the read-along chapter: the components, the symbols, the word current used in a sentence each.

On Thursday they start the lesson holding the vocabulary the explanation stands on, and the teacher checks their understanding first. If two terms of exit tickets show no difference, the evaluation will say so.

Why pre-teaching comes first

Pre-teaching is the low-literacy pupil's way in. Before the lesson the pupil hears and reads the words the lesson will rely on, with each sentence highlighted as the device's own voice reads it. Nothing needs installing and no account is needed.

It is also the action that can be tested most cleanly: exit-ticket results for pre-taught pupils against their peers, over two terms, decide whether it is scaled or stopped. That is why Electric Circuits is one of the five exemplar units.

Where SEND reform is going

Layers of support, and where AI fits

The February 2026 schools white paper replaces much of the current system with layers of support. Most of it lands from 2029. The DfE's 2026 support materials say AI may help draft initial support plans and staff guides, with great care over sensitive information, and must not write statutory documents without full professional review.

Most pupils

Universal offer

The teaching and adjustments every pupil can expect. This is where the adaptive pages sit: the same content for every pupil, with the help made visible to those who need it.

Pupils who need more

Individual support plan

Written by the school. AI may help draft from anonymised context sheets that list needs and strategies, never names; decisions stay with people.

Specialist provision

Education, health and care plan

Kept for specialist provision. Its content is on the boundary: AI does not write it.
