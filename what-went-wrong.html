§ 08The error log · kept in public

What went wrong

Every error found so far in building and publishing AI-drafted curriculum material, with what it could have cost, what was done and what changed in the process. Dated, newest first, and never deleted.

Why keep it

A leader who publishes his own audit

Adaptive by Design argues that a strategy which only reports its successes cannot be trusted with its claims, and its seventh recommendation is to report to governors what did not work alongside what did. That argument applies to its author first. Most of the material in an ecosystem like this is drafted with AI and edited by a subject specialist, at a scale of two thousand lessons, and some of it was wrong before it was caught. A governance position that cannot say where is not a position.

The debate is moving the same way. Sir Anthony Seldon and Tim Bunting's report of 22 September 2026 proposes governor-approved AI strategies in every school, and inspection of them. That is a proposal, not policy. If it comes, the first question worth asking of any school's AI governance is what has gone wrong and what was done about it. This page is my answer, kept where anyone can check it.

The log

Entries, newest first

Each entry records four things: what happened, what it could have cost, what was done, and what changed in the process so that it does not happen the same way twice. An entry is marked open while the correction is still outstanding.

  1. Ongoing
    Open

    The time AI saves is being absorbed

    What happened
    The teacher surveys predicted it: among teachers using AI, 51% said it had eased their workload, but only 35% were working fewer hours (BETT / YouGov, reported by Schools Week, 27 August 2026). In this work too, the hours AI has saved have so far been absorbed by building more.
    What it could have cost
    A year in which AI "saved time" and nobody could say where that time went, which is the position most schools are in and the one governors cannot question.
    What was done
    The strategy now names the destination of saved time in advance, and names it in terms of pupils: pre-teaching groups, feedback on extended writing, and the termly review of pupils on SEN Support.
    What changed in the process
    Where the saved time goes is a decision taken before rollout, not a finding reported after it. Governors can ask one precise question in July: where did the time go? Staff time is one of the registered measures.
  2. 17 October 2026
    Closed

    A build brief got the physics wrong

    What happened
    The brief written for the interactive circuit widget stated the currents in a parallel circuit wrongly. For 6 V across two 3 Ω lamps in parallel it said 2 A in total and 1 A in each branch. The correct values are 2 A in each branch and 4 A in total.
    What it could have cost
    A physics widget, built to let pupils see what the textbook describes, showing them the wrong numbers in a GCSE unit.
    What was done
    The widget's own self-test, which recomputes every preset circuit from the series and parallel rules, disagreed with the brief. The builder followed the self-test and shipped the correct values.
    What changed in the process
    The self-test, not the brief, is the authority. Anything with a right answer is checked by recomputation, never by agreement with the instructions it was built from.
  3. 17 October 2026
    Open

    The Macbeth deck teaches "counterfoil"

    What happened
    The English department's Macbeth deck (lesson 23) uses "counterfoil" for a character who contrasts with another. The literary term is "foil"; a counterfoil is the stub of a cheque or ticket.
    What it could have cost
    The wrong term carried into the unit, the read-along chapter and six home-language editions, and learnt by pupils as examination vocabulary.
    What was done
    The unit teaches "foil" and notes the deck's word. The correction was carried through the English text and all six translations. The deck is flagged to the department for correction.
    What changed in the process
    "Nothing appears that is not on the board" is a rule about scope, not a rule about truth. A deck is the source for what is taught, and still gets checked for whether it is right; what the check finds goes back to the department as a deck correction.
  4. 16 October 2026
    Closed

    The booklet count was reported as 170

    What happened
    The SEND booklet programme had been described as 170 booklets, the tally recorded on 9 September 2026. A recount from the department manifests on 16 October gave 222 booklets and 2,080 lessons. The earlier figure dated from before the Maths Years 8 to 11 build and had been carried forward from memory.
    What it could have cost
    A headline figure, in a leadership application and on this site, that nobody could trace to a count.
    What was done
    The count was redone from the manifests, one file per department, and the figures on this site and in the Leading SEND pack were replaced: 222 booklets, 2,080 lessons, 4,160 pages across seven departments.
    What changed in the process
    Counts come from the manifests, not from memory. Any total quoted in a document is recomputed from them on the day the document is finished.
  5. 16 October 2026
    Closed

    A quotation that the source does not contain

    What happened
    The proposal for leading SEND at Harlington, written in July 2026, said the House of Commons Library had called the schools white paper "the most significant shift in SEND policy in a decade". The current Commons Library briefing (CBP-10550, 1 September 2026) does not contain that sentence. The same proposal still said the DSG statutory override ended in March 2026, when its extension to March 2028 had been announced on 20 June 2025, more than a year before the proposal was written.
    What it could have cost
    A claim attributed to Parliament's own research service, written for a panel, that the service had not made; and a funding risk dated wrongly in a proposal, a panel presentation and a risk register.
    What was done
    The quotation was removed and the section rewritten from the white paper, the consultation and the briefing as they stand, with each proposal mapped to an action in the plan. The DSG date was corrected in the proposal, the presentation and the workbook. Every change is listed in the pack's change log with its source.
    What changed in the process
    A quotation is checked against the page it is attributed to on the day the document is finalised, and a document that names a date is re-read against the current guidance before it is reused.
  6. September 2026
    Open

    Home-language editions written by AI, not yet checked by a speaker

    What happened
    In September 2026 editions of a Year 8 English read-along were produced in Arabic, Urdu and Ukrainian. The translations were written by AI and had not been checked by a speaker of the language.
    What it could have cost
    Pupils with English as an additional language reading a wrong rendering of a key term as if it were the school's considered support, with nobody in the room able to tell.
    What was done
    Each page carries the notice "machine translation, not yet checked by a speaker" until a speaker signs it off, and none of it is in use with pupils as checked work.
    What changed in the process
    A translation is marked unchecked by default, and a page cannot drop the notice until a named speaker has signed the language off.

    Update, 17 October 2026. The exemplar units now carry home-language lines in six languages: Arabic, Urdu, Ukrainian, Portuguese, Polish and Somali. The same rule applies, and a speaker review sheet, kept beside the language files, lists, language by language, what each translation was least sure of so the check starts in the right places. The English stays the text that is read aloud; the home-language line sits under it.

  7. September 2026
    Closed

    Answer panels pasted over question panels

    What happened
    The Maths department's slides carry their answer panels pasted on top of the question panels of the graded worksheets. Read as text, a third or more of some worksheets was hidden, and what could be read was the answer, not the question.
    What it could have cost
    Booklets for pupils with SEND with a third of each worksheet missing, and questions reconstructed from their answers. Some had already shipped: in one unit two divisions had been read wrongly off the answer panel (438 ÷ 3 had become 4.38 ÷ 3), and in another an italic f had been read as a 7.
    What was done
    The question and answer panels are separate images inside each file, so the questions were recovered by reading those images directly. Every unit was put back through the recovery, and the shipped errors were corrected.
    What changed in the process
    Never read a question off its answer. Where the text of a slide does not carry its meaning, as it does not in maths, the slide image is the source and the text is a cross-check.
  8. August 2026
    Open

    Past-paper wording on public pages

    What happened
    An audit of the published A-level pages found some carrying past-paper wording, which exam-board terms do not allow on a public site.
    What it could have cost
    A breach of the examination board's terms for public sites, on pages already in use.
    What was done
    Correcting the affected pages is the first action of any implementation plan built on this work, and the strategy records the audit rather than leaving it out. The position going forward is that exam-board questions, mark schemes and licensed extracts stay behind the school sign-in.
    What changed in the process
    A checking tool now runs before anything is published. This entry is the reason the page exists: a strategy that only reports its successes cannot be trusted with its claims.
Corrections

How to report an error

If you find an error on this site or in any of the units, the quickest route is a message on LinkedIn. There is no form on this site and nothing you type here is sent anywhere. A correction that is upheld is added to this log as a dated entry, with what changed.
